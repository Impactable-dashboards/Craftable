01 — The Field

The landscape at a glance

Ten platforms map cleanly into three camps. Each is strong in its own corner and absent from the others. The center, a modern and beverage-led back office spanning restaurants, bars, and properties, is where none of them sit.

Camp 1 · Restaurant-first back-office platforms
Restaurant365MarginEdgeMarketManCrunchtimextraCHEF by Toast

Modern and operator-facing, strong on food cost, inventory, and AP. Built for restaurants, and they treat beverage as a subset of food. No hotel or property motion.

Camp 2 · Enterprise hotel procurement & operations suites
BirchStreetFourthAdacoHotSchedules

These do serve hotels, resorts, and casinos, at Marriott, Hilton, and Hyatt scale, with deep procurement, accounts payable, and, for Fourth, labor. They are enterprise-first, procurement or labor-led rather than beverage-led, older-generation in parts, and priced and scoped away from the mid-market.

Camp 3 · Beverage & bar inventory specialists
WISKBevSpotBackbarSculpture HospitalityBar-i

These own bar inventory, pour cost, and liquor counting, some with real depth. They are inventory-and-counting point tools, not a full financial back office, and most stop short of AP automation, property accounting, and multi-outlet roll-up.

The empty center

A modern, beverage-led, prescriptive back office that spans restaurants, bars, and properties, sits on top of the systems already in place, and serves the mid-market rather than only the enterprise. That center is Craftable's to own.

Also in the field: Yellow Dog Software (retail, food and beverage, and concessions inventory for resorts, casinos, stadiums, and hotels), Sculpture Hospitality and Bar-i (managed and scale-based beverage counting), Partender (fast bar counts), Apicbase and Growzer (European food and beverage management, including hotels), Lightspeed and TouchBistro (point-of-sale-native inventory), and newer AI back-of-house tools such as Nory and Restoke.

04 — White Space

The gaps, in sequence

What nobody in the field is saying, read against the full landscape. Strongest as one connected position: beverage depth, inside a full back office, for multi-outlet mid-market hospitality, on top of the existing stack, and prescriptive rather than just descriptive.

Gap 1 · Beverage depth inside a full back office

Opportunity: High
The gapThe restaurant platforms treat the bar as a subset of food. The tools that treat beverage as first-class, WISK, BevSpot, and Backbar, are inventory-and-counting point tools without AP automation, property accounting, or full financials. No one combines specialist beverage depth with a complete back office.
Why it mattersA large share of variance and shrinkage lives behind the bar, and it behaves differently from food. Operators either bolt a bar tool onto a food platform or run beverage in a silo. The single-platform answer is missing.
EvidenceCraftable's Bevager heritage; third-party reviewers place Bevager in the bar-inventory comparison set, which confirms the beverage depth is real and recognized, and Craftable carries it into a full platform (review sites, competitor sites).
Read: the most ownable lane in the field, and the clearest one to bring to the front.

Gap 2 · A modern, mid-market back office for hotel and multi-outlet F&B

Opportunity: High
The gapHotels are served, but by enterprise procurement suites. BirchStreet and Fourth's Adaco run hotel F&B procurement at Marriott and Hilton scale, procurement and labor-led, older-generation in parts, and priced for the enterprise. The modern, operator-facing, beverage-aware back office for the mid-market property is unbuilt.
Why it mattersMid-market hotels, resorts, casinos, and multi-outlet groups run many concepts under one entity but sit below the enterprise procurement tier and above the single-restaurant tools. They are underserved from both directions.
EvidenceCraftable already serves Kimpton, Pyramid Global, CoralTree, Hotel Swexan, bartaco, and PopStroke; BirchStreet and Fourth center on enterprise hotel groups and exclude the mid-market (competitor sites, case studies).
Read: defensible because it is proof-backed and honestly bounded against named incumbents.

Gap 3 · Tell operators what to do, not just what happened

Opportunity: Medium to high
The gapThe restaurant platforms compete on visibility, the hotel suites on procurement and spend, and the beverage tools on counting. Almost none frame the product around the next action to take.
Why it mattersAn operator running several outlets does not need another dashboard. They need a ranked list of what to fix today.
EvidenceCraftable's Operator AI already frames ranked daily actions before they hit the P&L (site).
Read: the durable version is ranked actions for a multi-outlet, beverage-led operation, not generic AI, which is already crowding in.

Gap 4 · Mixed-concept complexity under one modern roof

Opportunity: Medium
The gapEnterprise suites handle many properties as procurement; restaurant tools are per-location. The mid-market group running a restaurant, a bar, room service, and an entertainment venue still lacks one modern operator-facing spine across all of it.
Why it mattersThese groups need one purchasing and AP spine across every outlet, not four instances of a single-restaurant tool or an enterprise procurement rollout.
EvidenceCraftable's base includes food-and-entertainment and multi-outlet hospitality such as PopStroke, resorts, and casinos (site, case studies).
Read: the property structure itself is the moat the other camps have not built for in the mid-market.

Gap 5 · Sit on top of the stack, do not force a migration

Opportunity: Medium
The gapRestaurant365's model is to become your accounting system; enterprise suites arrive as multi-month implementations. No one makes a clear virtue of keeping the operator's existing accounting, ERP, and payments and layering on top.
Why it mattersMany groups will not rip out a working ledger or run an enterprise procurement project. Integrating with what they already run removes the biggest reason a deal stalls.
EvidenceCraftable cites 1,000+ integrations, M3 for hotels, and the Finexio payments partnership; Restaurant365 reviews flag implementation weight (sites, reviews).
Read: "keep your ledger, we sit on top" answers the top reason a migration-based deal slows.

09 — Takeaways

What this means

  • The field is three camps that do not overlap: restaurant-first platforms, enterprise hotel procurement suites, and beverage point tools. The center is empty.
  • Beverage is not unclaimed, but no one pairs specialist beverage depth with a full back office. That combination is Craftable's most ownable lane, and the one to lead with ahead of the AI framing the whole category shares.
  • Hotels are not unclaimed either. BirchStreet and Fourth own enterprise hotel F&B procurement. The modern, mid-market, beverage-aware property back office is the open lane, and naming those incumbents makes the claim credible.
  • Real-time visibility and AI are saturated across all three camps. Leading there is a losing hand.
  • The clearest position is the sequence: beverage depth, inside a full back office, for multi-outlet mid-market hospitality, on top of the existing stack, and prescriptive. Not any single claim.
  • Restaurant365 is most exposed on price and ledger lock-in; the beverage tools on completeness; the hotel suites on modernity and mid-market fit; Crunchtime on chain-only orientation.
  • The multi-stakeholder committee Craftable already names, Operations, Finance, Technology, and Accounting, points to an account-based go-to-market, developed in the Targeting analysis.

04 — Influenced Revenue

Because Craftable runs HubSpot, this ties to pipeline

Campaign Manager reports impressions and clicks and cannot see HubSpot, so on its own it cannot answer whether LinkedIn brought any deals. Authorize HubSpot once and that changes.

What it produces
  • Influenced pipeline, closed-won and closed-lost in one view, for deals where the account had meaningful ad activity before the deal was created. Craftable chooses what counts and sets the window to its real sales cycle.
  • A campaign influence score for scale-or-cut decisions, and which audiences produce pipeline versus only clicks, split by industry, headcount, and geography.
  • Account-level journeys tying anonymous site visits back to named companies and then to the deal record.
The feature to demo

Hidden pipeline. Accounts with heavy ad engagement and no deal record at all, surfaced automatically and exportable to sales, nurture, or outreach. For a lean go-to-market team this is often the most useful list in the system.

Honest caveat: reporting is only as good as the conversion capture underneath it, so events are defined and verified in week one before anything spends.
